Winter gives Jiuzhaigou a peculiar silence. It is not the absence of sound, but a gathering-in of sound by snow, trees, and water. The streams continue on their way; the wind still passes among the branches. Yet nothing speaks with summer’s haste.

This is my first photographic collection. I chose it as the beginning of the site’s photography not because it is flawless, but because it knows what it wishes to remember: those small, lucid, slightly distant moments in the natural world.

## The Blue Water

The water announces itself first. Cold does not diminish its brightness; against snow and pale grass, the lakes appear clearer and more remote. Blue-green deepens from the shallows like glass patiently polished by light.

In some frames, water occupies nearly the whole field of vision. Trees, snow, and stone withdraw to the margins until colour alone remains. Such an image requires little explanation. It is closer to a pause.

## Forest and Stream

Elsewhere the photographs enter the woods. Dark trunks, wet stone, and the last unmelted snow make the streams seem brighter. Water turns between rocks; white foam catches briefly in shadow and is gone.

I am drawn to this contrast: trees holding still while water refuses stillness. Winter lowers the volume of every colour, and whatever continues to move becomes newly visible.

## Mountains and Distance

The mountain photographs carry another sense of scale. Before a mountain, a person grows small, and many daily urgencies shrink with them. Cloud and light conceal part of the distant ridge, making it resemble memory: the details are incomplete, but the weight remains.

This collection is neither a guide nor a catalogue of landmarks. It is a record of winter, distance, colour, and the changing act of looking.

Later I may see all that could be improved—the framing, exposure, sequence, and the breath between images. That, too, belongs to a first collection. It is not a conclusion. It is a door left open.
